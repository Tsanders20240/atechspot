06 / BEFORE YOU FLY

Make the buying decision with the operation in mind.

ATechSpot is not a law firm or aviation authority. The FAA is the source of truth for U.S. operating requirements, registration, certification and airspace rules.

U.S. recreational / business basics

  • The FAA says drones at or above 250 g generally must be registered for recreational operations; Part 107 operations require registration regardless of weight.
  • Registered drones must comply with Remote ID unless an applicable exception applies.
  • Non-recreational or business operations generally fall under Part 107 or another applicable FAA pathway.
  • Airspace, visual-line-of-sight, people/vehicle operations and local restrictions can change what is lawful for a specific flight.

07 / QUICK ANSWERS

Know what you're buying—and who you're buying it from.

Is ATechSpot DJI an official DJI website?

No. ATechSpot DJI is an independent buying-guide and decision-tool property within the ATechSpot ecosystem. It is not owned, operated or endorsed by DJI unless an explicit written relationship is stated.

Why doesn't ATechSpot show a fixed product price?

Because price, promotions, inventory, bundles, shipping and regional availability can change quickly. The external seller page is the transaction source of truth. ATechSpot focuses on product fit and decision support.

Which model is best for real-estate photography?

Our default starting recommendation is Air 3S because its dual-camera approach provides useful framing flexibility. Mavic 4 Pro is the step-up when the business can justify flagship imaging capability. The right answer still depends on deliverables, flight environment, budget and operator requirements.

Which model is best for a beginner?

For low-friction personal capture, ATechSpot starts with Neo 2. For a more traditional creator-drone experience with a guarded design, look at Flip. For travel creators prioritizing stronger imaging and a compact kit, step to Mini 5 Pro.

Can I use a DJI drone for paid client work?

Potentially, but the aircraft is only one part of the decision. U.S. non-recreational operations generally require compliance with Part 107 or another applicable FAA pathway. Check the current FAA rules and the airspace for each operation.
