03 / ROOF SHOT PLAN

Capture the roof systematically.

A repeatable shot list makes the flight more useful to estimators, roofing crews and clients than random cinematic passes.

Property context

Capture the full structure, roof geometry, adjacent trees, utilities and access context before moving to details.

Each roof plane

Document every major slope or plane with consistent orientation so the viewer can understand coverage without guessing.

Ridges, hips & valleys

Capture intersections and drainage paths as visual documentation. Do not label a condition as defective unless a qualified professional makes that determination.

Flashing & penetrations

Frame chimneys, vents, skylights and other penetrations with enough surrounding context to show their location.

Gutters & drainage

Document visible gutters, downspouts and drainage areas when relevant to the project scope.

Detail + context pair

For any area of interest, capture one tighter image and one wider reference image so the location can be identified later.

05 / FLIGHT + SCOPE CHECK

Know what the drone can and cannot prove.

ATechSpot is not an aviation authority, roofing inspector, engineer, insurer or law firm. Use the FAA and relevant qualified professionals as the source of truth for their respective domains.

FAA Part 107 / airspace

Work, business and other non-recreational drone operations in the United States generally follow Part 107 or another applicable FAA pathway. Part 107 includes operational requirements such as visual line of sight, avoiding careless or reckless operation and restrictions around people. Controlled-airspace operations may require authorization.

Not thermal. Not structural diagnosis.

Mini 5 Pro and Mavic 4 Pro are camera drones. This page is about visual documentation. They do not by themselves determine hidden moisture, thermal anomalies, structural soundness, building-code compliance, insurability or whether a roof must be replaced. Use the appropriate qualified inspector, roofer, engineer, thermal equipment or other method when the job requires those conclusions.

06 / FAQ

Roofing drone questions.

Which DJI drone would ATechSpot choose for a professional roof-inspection business?

Mavic 4 Pro is the current ATechSpot professional visual-documentation pick because the seller-listed camera system includes wide, 70mm and 168mm focal-length options. That gives a professional operator more framing flexibility. It is still a camera platform, not a substitute for thermal or structural inspection methods.

Is Mini 5 Pro enough for roofing contractors?

For many overview, estimate and routine documentation workflows, yes. ATechSpot prefers it when portability and fast field deployment matter more than dedicated tele-camera options.

Can I call drone photos a roof inspection report?

Be precise about your scope. Visual images can support an inspection or estimate, but do not imply that photographs establish structural, moisture, code, engineering, insurance or thermal conclusions unless the qualified person and appropriate method support those conclusions.
