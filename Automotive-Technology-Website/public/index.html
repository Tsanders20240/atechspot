02 · THE AUTOMOTIVE TECH STACK

Seven systems shape the modern driving experience.

Understanding these layers helps you separate a marketing name from what the vehicle actually does.

01

Sensors

Cameras, radar, ultrasonic sensors and other inputs help the vehicle perceive nearby lanes, objects and conditions.

02

Driver assistance

Warnings and interventions can support braking, steering, lane position and distance management.

03

Powertrain

Gas, hybrid, plug-in hybrid and battery-electric vehicles use different energy and control systems.

04

Diagnostics

Vehicle modules store fault information and alerts that help technicians investigate problems.

05

Connectivity

Bluetooth, cellular links, Wi-Fi, mobile apps and cloud services connect the vehicle to devices and accounts.

06

Software

Infotainment, control modules and over-the-air updates increasingly shape vehicle behavior and features.

07

Human interface

Dashboards, alerts, touchscreens and controls determine how clearly the car communicates with the driver.

03 · DRIVER ASSISTANCE / ADAS

Assistance can help. You still drive.

NHTSA distinguishes warnings, collision intervention and driving-control assistance. Feature names vary by manufacturer, so learn the function instead of relying on the brand name.

WARNING

Forward Collision Warning

Warns the driver when a potential frontal collision is detected. A warning system by itself does not perform the avoidance maneuver for you.

INTERVENTION

Automatic Emergency Braking

Can automatically apply braking when a forward collision is imminent, depending on the system and conditions.

DRIVING ASSISTANCE

Adaptive Cruise Control

Adjusts speed to help maintain a selected following distance from a vehicle ahead.

DRIVING ASSISTANCE

Lane Centering

Provides continuous steering assistance to help keep the vehicle centered in its lane while the driver remains engaged.

Important:NHTSA says Level 0–2 features require the driver to remain responsible and attentive. No vehicle currently sold to consumers in the U.S. is fully self-driving.NHTSA driver-assistance guide ↗

04 · ELECTRIC VEHICLES

Think in charging habits, not just range.

An EV decision is easier when you start with where the vehicle sleeps, how far you drive, how often you road-trip and what charging access you actually have.

HOME + PUBLIC CHARGING
Daily drivingStart with your normal commute and errands.
Home accessKnow whether you can reliably charge where you park.
Road tripsCheck compatible fast-charging networks along real routes.
WeatherTemperature, speed and HVAC use can affect energy consumption.

Questions to ask before buying

  • Can I charge at home or at work?
  • What connector/charging standard does the exact vehicle use?
  • What charging speed can the vehicle accept?
  • What is covered by the battery warranty?
  • How does the navigation system plan charging stops?
  • What software or subscription features are tied to ownership?

05 · OBD-II + DASHBOARD ALERTS

A code is a clue, not a repair answer.

Diagnostic trouble codes can point a technician toward a system or condition. They do not automatically identify the failed part or justify replacing one.

1

Read the exact alert

Note the warning light, message, driving symptoms and when the issue occurs.

2

Check the owner’s manual

Some warnings require immediate action; others allow cautious driving to service.

3

Scan when appropriate

OBD-II data can help document fault codes and system information for further diagnosis.

4

Verify before replacing parts

Use testing and service information to confirm the cause instead of treating a code as a parts list.

Safety rule: If the vehicle displays a red warning, overheating, loss of braking/steering, smoke, fire risk, or another immediate safety concern, stop using the vehicle as safely as possible and follow the manufacturer/emergency guidance.

06 · CONNECTED CAR + PRIVACY

Your vehicle may have an account lifecycle.

Modern cars can store paired phones, location history, garage access, app permissions, driver profiles and subscription settings. Treat ownership transfer like a technology handoff.

Before selling or trading

Remove paired devices, profiles, navigation destinations, garage access, digital keys and app/account connections. Follow the manufacturer’s reset process.

After buying used

Confirm the previous owner is removed from connected services and digital keys. Change account credentials and review app permissions.

Before adding an app

Review what data the app can access, whether remote controls are enabled, and whether a subscription is required later.

07 · SMART VEHICLE BUYER CHECKLIST

Test the technology before you finance it.

Do not assume a feature name means the same thing across brands or model years. Verify the exact VIN/equipment and try the system yourself.
